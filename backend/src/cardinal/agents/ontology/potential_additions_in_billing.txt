Potential additions to the Cardinal billing ontology

Purpose
=======

These additions would make the billing ontology complete enough to support a
credible credit-card billing simulation. The current workbook is structurally
sound as a first draft, but several formulas refer to facts that have not yet
been defined.


1. Billing dates and cycle timing
=================================

Add fields for:

- Cycle start date
- Cycle end date
- Statement date
- Payment due date
- Transaction date
- Posting date
- Payment received date
- Number of days in the billing cycle

Why this is needed:

Purchases and payments affect interest differently depending on when they occur.
The model cannot calculate a true daily balance or determine whether a payment
was made on time using monthly totals alone.

The ontology should also define exactly what cycle index t means. For example,
a payment received during cycle t may be paying the statement produced at the
end of cycle t-1.


2. Missing balance components
=============================

Add fields for:

- Previous cycle ending balance
- Beginning balance
- Cash advances
- Fees
- Credits
- Refunds
- Adjustments
- Charge-offs
- Reversed transactions

Why this is needed:

The current ending-balance formula already mentions several of these values,
but they are not defined as fields. Every value used by a formula should exist
in the Fields sheet and have a corresponding dependency row.

A basic recurrence should be explicitly defined as:

  ending balance
  = beginning balance
  + purchases
  + cash advances
  + fees
  + interest
  - payments
  - credits
  - charge-offs
  +/- adjustments


3. Daily balance calculation
============================

Add a daily account-balance concept or a daily ledger projection.

It should explain how each day's balance is produced from:

- Beginning balance
- Purchases posted that day
- Cash advances posted that day
- Payments received or posted that day
- Credits and refunds posted that day
- Fees and adjustments posted that day

Why this is needed:

Average daily balance requires a sequence of daily balances. It cannot be
calculated accurately from total monthly purchases and payments unless the
ontology declares a simplifying timing assumption.


4. Separate balance types
=========================

Add balance categories for:

- Purchase balance
- Cash-advance balance
- Balance-transfer balance
- Promotional balance
- Fee balance, if modeled separately
- Interest balance, if modeled separately

Why this is needed:

Different balance types can have different APRs, grace-period treatment,
promotional periods, and payment-allocation rules. One account-level APR and
one combined balance cannot represent those differences.


5. Interest configuration
=========================

Add fields or policy settings for:

- Interest calculation method
- Daily periodic rate
- Monthly periodic rate
- APR effective start and end dates
- Purchase APR
- Cash-advance APR
- Balance-transfer APR
- Promotional APR
- Penalty APR
- Minimum-interest charge
- Interest rounding method
- Compounding or capitalization rule
- Number of days used to convert APR to a daily rate

Why this is needed:

The current formula assumes average_daily_balance * APR / 12. That works only
when the product uses a monthly periodic rate. If it uses a daily periodic rate,
the calculation generally also needs the number of days in the cycle.


6. Complete grace-period logic
==============================

Add fields and rules for:

- Whether the product offers a grace period
- Which balance types are eligible
- Grace-period length
- Balance that must be paid to preserve grace
- Deadline by which that payment must be received
- Whether grace was available at the start of the cycle
- Whether grace was lost during the cycle
- Whether grace can be restored, and under what conditions

Why this is needed:

Grace eligibility is more than a single paid-in-full flag. It depends on product
terms, balance type, payment timing, and the balance that the customer was
required to pay.

The meaning of paid_in_full_last_cycle should be made precise. It should state
which statement balance is being tested and which payments, through which due
date, count toward the test.


7. Minimum-payment and payment behavior
=======================================

Add policy fields for:

- Minimum-payment percentage
- Minimum-payment floor amount
- Treatment of billed fees
- Treatment of billed interest
- Past-due amount
- Amount over the credit limit
- Fixed-plan or promotional installment amount
- Rule that caps the minimum payment at the statement balance

Add account-cycle fields for:

- Minimum payment due
- Amount paid by the due date
- Payment status: full, minimum, partial, missed, or overpayment
- Late payment indicator
- Returned payment amount
- Past-due amount
- Days past due

Why this is needed:

The minimum-payment formula is an issuer policy, so its parameters must be
explicit. Payment generation should also depend on the statement balance,
minimum payment, due date, and customer payment behavior rather than being an
unconnected random draw.


8. Payment allocation
=====================

Add rules describing how a payment is divided among:

- Purchase balances
- Cash-advance balances
- Balance-transfer balances
- Promotional balances
- Fees and interest
- Amounts with different APRs

Why this is needed:

Payment allocation changes the balances that remain and therefore changes
future interest. Without it, a multi-rate account will not reconcile correctly.


9. Missing dependency edges
============================

Add dependency rows connecting:

- Previous ending balance to current beginning balance
- Beginning balance to daily balance
- Dated transactions and payments to daily balance
- Daily balance to average daily balance
- Applicable APR, periodic rate, cycle days, and grace state to interest
- Interest to cycle ending balance
- Cash advances to cycle ending balance
- Fees to cycle ending balance
- Credits and refunds to cycle ending balance
- Charge-offs and adjustments to cycle ending balance
- Statement balance, fees, interest, past-due amount, policy percentage, and
  policy floor to minimum payment due
- Statement balance, minimum payment, customer behavior, and due date to payment
- Payment allocation to the ending balance of each balance type

Every variable used in a formula should have an explicit dependency edge.


10. Additional invariants
=========================

Add hard validation rules for:

- Ending balance reconciles to beginning balance and all posted flows
- Statement balance reconciles to the ledger as of the statement date
- Sum of balance-type balances equals the total balance
- Average daily balance reconciles to daily balances
- Interest charged is not negative
- Payments, purchases, fees, and credits follow their declared bounds
- Grace-eligible purchase balances do not receive prohibited interest
- Minimum payment is not negative
- Minimum payment does not exceed statement balance or the amount legally due
- Amount paid by the due date is calculated only from eligible payments
- Payment allocations sum to the total payment
- Closed or charged-off accounts do not receive unsupported new activity

Add soft checks for unusual but possible results, such as exceptionally large
payments, unusually high utilization, or repeated returned payments.


11. Initial conditions and exceptional events
=============================================

Define behavior for:

- The first billing cycle, where no previous statement exists
- Newly opened accounts
- Zero-balance accounts
- Credit balances
- Closed accounts
- Suspended accounts
- Delinquent accounts
- Charged-off accounts
- Refunds and disputes
- Reversed purchases and payments
- Returned payments
- Late fees
- Over-limit amounts
- Account hardship or payment plans

Why this is needed:

Formulas that use t-1 require a defined starting state. Exceptional events also
need explicit treatment so they do not accidentally break reconciliation.


12. Precise definitions and naming
==================================

Define these terms unambiguously:

- payment[t]
- statement_balance[t]
- previous balance
- beginning balance
- cycle ending balance
- statement balance
- revolves
- paid in full
- grace eligible
- payment received date
- payment posting date
- transaction date
- posting date

Use field IDs consistently inside formulas and invariant expressions. For
example, formulas should not mix bal, interest, and f_interest_charged unless a
documented expression language maps those names.


13. Source and regulatory precision
====================================

For every regulatory item:

- Cite the exact subsection instead of a broad section when possible
- Separate legal requirements from issuer policy
- Separate statement-disclosure requirements from calculation rules
- Remove or source quantitative claims such as estimated revenue impact
- Record the date or version of the regulatory source reviewed
- Have a qualified compliance specialist confirm the final interpretation

For example, a minimum-payment formula is normally issuer policy, while rules
about what must appear on a periodic statement are regulatory obligations.
Those should be represented as separate ontology facts.


14. Workbook and Turtle synchronization
=======================================

Update the workbook-to-Turtle process so billing.ttl contains every:

- Grain
- Field
- Dependency, including lag and mechanism
- Invariant
- Obligation
- Citation

Add an automated comparison test that fails when the workbook and billing.ttl
describe different IDs or dependency edges.

Also declare every ontology property used by the Turtle file, including
card:grainKey, and expand the SHACL checks to cover required fields, enum values,
reference integrity, dependency cycles, and obligation references.


Recommended implementation order
================================

1. Define dates, cycle timing, and t versus t-1 semantics.
2. Add all missing balance components and dependency edges.
3. Add daily balances and average-daily-balance calculation.
4. Define interest methods, APR types, and grace-period behavior.
5. Define minimum-payment policy, payment behavior, and allocation.
6. Add balance categories and exceptional account states.
7. Add the missing invariants.
8. Correct and verify regulatory citations.
9. Synchronize the workbook and Turtle ontology automatically.
